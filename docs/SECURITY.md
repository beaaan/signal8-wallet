# Security self-assessment

Required by the rules: permission tiers and a known-risk list. The red-team numbers in §5 are to be refreshed after the model rerun on the frozen cases (17 Oct).

## 1. Principle

The agent is controlled the way a bank controls a junior treasurer. Every control is a contract rule, not an instruction in a prompt, so losses stay bounded even if the model, its key or one data source is compromised.

## 2. Permission tiers

| Tier | What the agent may do | Who approves | Enforced by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L0 Read | Balances, signals, invoices, its own ledger | Nobody | n/a |
| L1 Draft | Draft plans, claims, proposals, messages | Nobody; nothing moves | Backend has no send path for drafts |
| L2 Act within plan | Pay payees registered for the active plan under per-payment, per-plan daily and overall daily caps; vault deposits and withdrawals (always back to the Safe); anchor reports; while storm mode is on, storm-plan actions: payroll, credit draw within the per-event cap, cover claim | The signed plan | `PlanModule` (typed functions only, no generic call) |
| L3 Co-sign | Anything above a cap; a payee not in the active plan (e.g., ordinary-day payroll); credit beyond the storm cap | Two of the three human Safe owners; the agent only proposes, as a Safe delegate | Safe threshold; the agent is not an owner |
| L4 Human only | Change plans, payees, limits, owners, modules, relayers | Safe (two owners) + 24 h timelock | `PlanModule` timelock, `PlanGuard` |

## 3. Bank controls mapped to contract rules

This table maps selected banking and treasury control concepts to the demo.
It distinguishes on-chain restrictions, backend checks and owner procedures.
It is not a claim of banking regulatory compliance or production readiness.
Each control reduces specific risks within the stated scope; it does not
eliminate fraud or verify every business obligation.

| Control | Prototype mechanism / layer | Risk mitigated | Current limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment and credit limits (支付及信贷限额管理) | `PlanModule` enforces per-payment, per-plan daily, overall payment and per-event credit limits. | Excessive payments or borrowing through the agent path. | The payment ceiling covers module payments, not every Safe movement. A fraudulent payment can still fit within a limit. |
| Maker-checker / dual approval (双人复核) | On Base Sepolia, the agent may create an outside-plan payment proposal as a Safe delegate. Executing that payment requires signatures from two of the three Safe owners. | Unilateral approval of exceptional payments. | Within-plan actions are pre-authorised, not individually checked by two humans. Independent owner custody is not established in the documented demo; see known risk 19. |
| Segregation of duties (职责分离) | Agent execution privileges are separated from owner administration privileges. | Agent privilege escalation. | Technical role separation is not a complete separation of payment, accounting and independent review functions. Deployment limitations remain in the known-risk list. |
| Payee-change delay | Subsequent payee changes pass through the owner-controlled 24-hour timelock. | Immediate activation of an unauthorised recipient change. | The delay is a project parameter, not identity verification. Bootstrap configuration bypasses it; removal also waits, so containment may require pausing. |
| Duplicate-payment control | The backend constructs invoice/payroll references; the module rejects reuse of a submitted reference. | Re-execution of the same referenced payment. | This is not complete invoice-balance accounting. Instalments and deposits are allowed only against a purchase order, each part with its own reference and bounded by what the order still allows (issue #16, known risk 21). Direct owner payments and reissued invoices rely on backend checks; see known risks 13 and 15. |
| Two-source corroboration | Storm activation pairs fresh local-weather and GDACS attestations from distinct registered relayer addresses. | Activation from a single forged source attestation. | Distinct addresses do not prove independent operators or accurate source data. This is a project-specific oracle safeguard, not a universal banking requirement. |
| Reconciliation and exception detection | At each period close the backend compares, at one block, recorded Safe/fund movements, module spending counters, the lender's outstanding principal and every token transfer into or out of the Safe against chain readings, and anchors a report built from that snapshot. | Unexplained differences within the compared scope, including movements that net to zero. | Vault share and lender events are checked by balance only; direct owner payments are accepted only with their transaction hash (issue #18, fixed; known risk 23). An unchecked report is not verified. |
| Emergency suspension | Safe, guardian or agent can request module pause without a timelock; only Safe can unpause. | Further agent actions through the paused module. | Confirmation depends on the network. Pause does not reverse settled payments or freeze every Safe/router path. Recovery requires owner action. |

### Additional operational controls — proposed

The following are recommended operational controls, not newly implemented
features or amendments to the signed policy. Owners must approve their
adoption and responsibilities.

| Recommended control | Current status | Proposed owner procedure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payee verification and change evidence | Registered addresses and a timelock do not establish the real recipient's identity. | Confirm new or changed payment details through a trusted contact channel independent of the request; retain verification and approval records. |
| Audit trail and evidence retention | Decision logs and report anchors exist, but anchoring does not establish completeness or preserve the underlying files. | Retain original reports, logs, run metadata and receipts; restrict modification and preserve recoverable copies. |
| Periodic limit and access review | Owners can change configuration; an automated review process is not established. | Record reviews before deployment, periodically at an owner-approved cadence and after material business or role changes. |
| Exception review and escalation | Proposals, refusals and inflow flags exist; complete follow-through is not established. | Record an owner responsible for investigating each material exception and retain its resolution, including direct owner payments. |
| Key custody and incident recovery | Independent owner custody and a fully tested recovery procedure are not established. | Separate owner-key custody; document emergency responsibilities and rehearse pause, investigation and authorised recovery. |

## 4. Threats and responses

| Threat | Response | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instructions hidden in emails or invoices | Extractor has no tools and outputs fields only; payee must already be registered; invoices are checked against purchase orders and paid invoices; credit notes and statements never start a payment; a document that itself says the goods are not received, disputed or on hold goes to the owners | A registered payee without a purchase order requirement could be overpaid up to the per-payment cap |
| Fake storm warning (email, Zalo) | Messages cannot unlock anything; only on-chain attestations can | None found |
| One relayer key compromised | Cannot unlock storm mode alone; the contract refuses to register one key for both sources | Two compromised keys → losses capped by the storm plan |
| Replayed or stale attestation | Nonce; signing time must be under 1 h old; HKO attestations lapse after 12 h, GDACS after 24 h | None found |
| Agent key stolen | Attacker gets L2 at most: payees in an active plan, under the caps. The agent is not a Safe owner, so it cannot sign anything else. Guardian pauses | Up to `dailyCeiling` per day to existing payees; credit draws up to the per-event cap, which land in the Safe |
| Over-borrowing in a storm | Credit drawn per storm event is capped by the storm plan and by the line; the event id is fixed when the storm starts, so a GDACS re-attestation with another id cannot reset the cap or claim the cover twice | Up to the per-event cap, drawn into the Safe. A stolen GDACS key plus the agent key could end and restart a storm under a new id for another cap; the money still lands in the Safe, as debt |
| Duplicate invoice or payroll run | Deterministic `ref` per payee and normalised invoice number, or per pay period; the contract rejects a used `ref`. A re-issue (same supplier and amount, same date or no date) goes to the owners. An invoice paid in parts (instalments, the balance after a deposit) is allowed only against a purchase order, each part bounded by what the order still allows and with its own one-use ref (part n) | An invoice re-issued under a new id with a new amount still needs a registered payee and fits the caps; see known risk 21 for part payments |
| The model planner steered to another registered payee (e.g. one without a purchase-order rule) | The payee must be the one the invoice names (its literal address or a name match); a payee only the model could match goes to the owners | None found |
| Arguments smuggled through an allowed call (e.g., vault `withdraw` to another receiver) | No generic call path; the module builds each call with the Safe as receiver | None found |
| Contract bugs | OpenZeppelin building blocks, unit, fuzz and invariant tests | Not audited |
| Personal data on-chain | Only hashes on-chain | None found |

## 6. Known-risk list

1. **Not audited.** Contracts are tested (unit, fuzz, invariant) but have had no external audit.
2. **Relayers are a trust point.** Two independent keys and sources reduce it; a decentralised oracle (e.g., Chainlink CRE) is the production path.
3. **Bootstrap configuration skips the timelock.** Initial plans, payees, the daily ceiling and the target contracts are set in the constructor; only later changes wait 24 h.
4. **Mock counterparties.** The insurer and lender are labelled simulations, not real products.
5. **Testnet only.** No real funds; the dollar token is a mock.
6. **Model output quality.** Wrong extraction can propose a wrong amount to a registered payee, bounded by the per-payment cap and visible in the decision log.
7. **Guard risk.** A faulty Safe guard can lock a Safe. One bound to a different Safe passes Safe's `setGuard` check and then rejects every transaction, so the throwaway script checks `guard.safe()` before setting it. `PlanGuard` is unit-tested and fork-tested on a fresh 2-of-3 Safe made through the real Safe v1.4.1 contracts on Base Sepolia (`contracts/test/PlanGuardFork.t.sol`, 15 tests): ordinary transfers and MultiSendCallOnly payroll batches pass; module, owner, threshold, guard and fallback changes, a batch hiding one, and any other delegatecall are blocked until queued for 24 h; a failed inner call keeps its queue entry; recovery is a queued `setGuard(address(0))`. Safe v1.4.1 does not run guards on module transactions, so `PlanModule` is unaffected and `enableModule` is itself guarded. The fork tests found that MultiSendCallOnly v1.5.0 sends a batch entry addressed to `address(0)` to the Safe itself; the guard now counts such an entry as a call to the Safe (14 Oct). The deployed guard `0x10A1…5F8e` is bound to v1.4.1, which does not do this, but it predates the fix: a guard set on the demo Safe should be deployed from the current source. **Set on a live throwaway Safe, not on the demo Safe.** On 14 Oct `contracts/script/ThrowawayGuard.s.sol` set a guard deployed from the current source on a new throwaway 1-of-1 Safe on Base Sepolia (`0x1C78…21A6`, guard `0xA3e1…7c65`): an ordinary Safe transaction passed through it, and `addOwnerWithThreshold` and `setGuard(address(0))` revert with `Blocked(selector)` against the live Safe (EVIDENCE.md). The demo Safe needs two of its owners' signatures and a guard deployed from the current source. Without the guard, two human owners acting together can change modules or owners at once; the agent alone cannot.
8. **Replay mode.** In the Ragasa and Yagi replays the relayers sign with the current time and keep the 2025 official time as `sourceTime`, and each run salts the GDACS event id so the cover and credit cap reset per run. Live mode uses the real event id and signs as soon as the source changes.
9. **Tightening changes are timelocked too.** Removing a payee or lowering a cap waits 24 h like any other change; the owner pauses the module in the meantime.
10. **Agent can pause itself.** A stolen agent key can stop the agent (denial of service) but cannot move money outside the plan.
11. **Local runs use a stand-in Safe.** On Anvil the Business Safe is `LocalSafe` (one admin, no signatures) so the demo runs without a Safe deployment; it is never deployed to Base Sepolia, where the real 2-of-3 Safe holds the funds. The local FISCO chain (Yagi) has no Safe either, so its wallet is also a `LocalSafe`: one owner key can move the funds and change the module, and there is no guard. `PlanModule`'s limits on the agent are the same there.
12. **Owner proposals are not yet tested against the live Safe service.** On Base Sepolia the agent, as a Safe delegate, proposes through the Safe Transaction Service (API Kit) and also logs each proposal to `agent/logs/proposals.jsonl`; this path is unit-tested with a fake service only. Either way the agent cannot sign them.
13. **Registered-payee abuse is checked off-chain, capped on-chain.** The agent checks invoices against purchase orders, a usual amount for payees without purchase orders (the landlord's rent), and paid invoices, and sends overpaid, re-issued or PO-less invoices to the owners; a duplicate invoice id is refused. These checks live in the backend, so a stolen agent key skips them: the contract still bounds the loss to registered payees under the caps. A payee with neither a purchase order nor a usual amount could still be overbilled within the per-payment cap; the red-team case `overpay-landlord-001` shows the usual amount closing that for the landlord. The finance members' 100-case corpus (`redteam/finance/`, 14 Oct) holds 100/100 attack runs with 50/50 benign scenarios on the rules provider, after the fixes for issue #16 (amount due over order totals, part payments, credit notes, statements, replacement invoices, holds). With the model (`deepseek-flash`, 14 Oct) attackers won 1 of 100 runs against us with no funds lost and 50/50 benign after a planner prompt fix (44/50 before it), against 5 of 100 and 4,820.00 lost for a bare agent (§5).
14. **Regulation.** Crypto is not a lawful means of payment in Vietnam; the Yagi replay uses a mock bank-issued VND deposit token (`MockVNDDeposit`, mVND) on a local FISCO BCOS consortium chain. Hong Kong licenses stablecoin issuers since 2026; this is a prototype, not a licensed service.
15. **Owner-signed payments do not mark refs in `PlanModule`.** When two owners sign a proposed payment it goes straight from the Safe, so the module never sees its ref. The agent keeps the refs it proposed (`logs/proposals.jsonl`, per PlanModule) and will neither pay nor re-propose them; a stolen agent key could still pay such an invoice once within the plan's caps.
16. **Queued changes do not expire.** A change queued in `PlanModule` or the attestor can be applied any time after its 24 h delay; owners cancel stale entries with `cancelChange`.
17. **The arena runs on its own local chain.** Arena submissions go through the real pipeline, so they use caps and refs; on demo day the arena's agent runs on a separate anvil (`docs/DEMO.md`), never the demo Safe.
18. **Two tools outside the payment path send document text to a model.** Untrusted text otherwise reaches only the extractor. The plan compiler (`npm run plan:compile -- --model`) reads the owners' policy and loan texts to draft a plan book, and the bare-agent baseline (`npm run redteam:bare`) gives the model the raw attack text by design. Neither has a key or touches a chain: the compiler returns JSON that must pass the plan-book schema, quote its sources verbatim and use only registered addresses, and stays a draft until two owners sign; the baseline's wallet is simulated. Kept by decision (bean, 10 Oct).
19. **The Base Sepolia owners are not yet three people.** The demo Safe's three owner keys were generated together as testnet keys and are held in one place, so the 2-of-3 currently protects against the agent, not against whoever holds that file. Before the demo, owners can be swapped to separate wallets with a Safe owner change (two signatures).
20. **The daily ceiling covers agent payments only.** `dailyCeiling` counts what `PlanModule.pay` sends to suppliers and staff. Vault moves, credit draws and cover payouts have their own limits, and payments two owners sign directly from the Safe never pass through the module. Policy 4.3 has the owners record such payments in the daily report; the agent's reconciliation finds an unrecorded one in the Safe's token transfer logs, even when two such movements cancel out in the balance, and pauses the module.
21. **Part payments are bounded by the purchase order, not by what is still owed.** An invoice can be paid in parts only when a purchase order gives its total, and the parts together never exceed it. Credits, discounts and rebates are not in the owners' records, so an invoice settled below its order (say 1,100 of a 1,200 order after a credit) could be topped up to the order total by a later invoice under the same number. The money still goes to the registered supplier, and never beyond what the owners approved for that order.
22. **"Not received" is only what the document says.** The agent sends an invoice to the owners when the document itself says the goods were not delivered, are disputed, or asks to hold payment, and untrusted text can only make a payment more cautious this way. There are no goods-received records yet, so an invoice that matches its purchase order and stays silent about delivery is paid even if nothing arrived. A three-way match (order, goods received, invoice) is the control a bank would expect; it needs the owners to record deliveries.
23. **What reconciliation checks, and what it does not.** At the close of each period (the ordinary day, and in the replay the storm period too) the agent compares, all at one block: the Safe's cash, its vault assets, `spentToday`, the lender's outstanding principal (against recorded draws minus router repayments), and every token transfer into or out of the Safe (each must be a transaction the ledger has). The report it anchors is built from that same snapshot. It does not read the vault's share events or the lender's own events (they are checked by balance), and the only way to account for a payment the owners made directly is its transaction hash. Anyone can send tokens to the Safe, which reads as an unrecorded movement and pauses the module: a nuisance pause, not a loss. After a confirmed mismatch the pause and the owner alert no longer depend on the report being written or anchored.
24. **The Yagi evidence is from a local FISCO chain.** Its hashes can be checked only on our node (4 nodes on one machine, one operator), not on a public explorer, and a rebuilt chain gives new ones. Two things there are workarounds rather than product: Foundry reaches FISCO v3.16.3 through a JSON-RPC shim that only pads the empty `logsBloom` in block headers (`contracts/fisco/rpc-shim.mjs`), and the relayer seals a fresh block with a 0-value transfer before attesting on an idle chain. Both chain-specific facts the contracts depend on were measured, not assumed: `block.timestamp` is in milliseconds (`timeUnit` 1000) and `CHAINID` returns 20200 (the attestor's EIP-712 chain id).
